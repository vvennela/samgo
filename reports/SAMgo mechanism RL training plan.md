# Ship Glimmer mechanisms with free SFT first

**Bottom line:** SAMgo can hit Nov 4 on Glimmer, but only if the free LoRA SFT does the main work and GRPO is a small, capped polish. At Fireworks serverless prices, $100 buys only about **22-48 GRPO steps** on Muse Glimmer 30B (about 3k-6k rollouts). That is a few percent of the RL volume the closest LEGO paper used. Every published recipe that worked (STABLE, CAD-Coder, BesiegeField, PVPO) did SFT on *ground-truth* data first, then RL with a gated reward that couples validity and fidelity. The project's own pilots point the same way: SFT on self-samples failed twice, and verifier-feedback retries beat best-of-k. So the plan has three parts. First, build (corrupted build -> verified fix) pairs from the algorithmic builder's own verified cars and SFT Glimmer on them for free before the Nov 30 cutoff. Second, run one GRPO pass of about 35 steps for about $60, with prompts cut to about 1k tokens. Third, rely on verifier-feedback retries at inference for the Camry demo. Switching to Llama or Qwen on rented GPUs buys 10-35x more RL steps per dollar. It costs days of infrastructure work, Llama is no longer on any managed per-token RL service, and it abandons the "Glimmer is the builder" premise of a Meta hackathon entry. With six days left, that trade is not worth it. The biggest risks are not budget. They are three things. The Camry still fails stability. The door and wheel verifiers are not yet built. Fireworks' RL billing has unverified multipliers, such as padding and possible extra logprob passes.

## $100 buys about 30 Glimmer GRPO steps, and prompt length decides the count

Fireworks' Serverless Training API supports GRPO with LoRA on `accounts/fireworks/models/muse-glimmer-30b`. Its live per-million-token rates are **prefill $1.96, cached prefill $0.39, sample $4.88 and train $5.86** ([Fireworks pricing](https://fireworks.ai/pricing)). The free promotion is **LoRA SFT only**. The cookbook's Glimmer README titles its RL step "RL on DeepMath (Serverless Training API, paid)" and says RL "is not covered by the promotion" ([fw-ai/cookbook](https://github.com/fw-ai/cookbook)). The cookbook pins the promotion's end to jobs started before 2026-12-01 Pacific. Fireworks publishes no RL cost formula and sends RL estimates to its Training team ([Fireworks contact-training](https://fireworks.ai/contact-training)). The arithmetic below is therefore ours.

For a step of 16 prompts × 8 samples, with a 3k-token prompt and a 550-token completion, one step costs about **$3.23**, so $100 buys about **31 steps**. The **train meter is about 82% of the cost**. This is because `forward_backward` bills the whole prompt plus completion for every rollout, so each prompt token is billed eight times per step. Prompt length is the lever that matters. Cutting the prompt from 3k to 1k tokens brings a step to about **$1.6**, or about 60 steps per $100. This matters a great deal for SAMgo. A 9,531-part Camry listed as JSON would be far longer than any affordable prompt, and probably longer than the 128K context. The edit task must therefore show the model a **local crop**: the wheel-arch region, the door panel, or the window band, plus a compact summary of the car. It cannot show the whole body. Three billing questions are open, and each could raise the real cost above the table: whether the 8 samples share one prompt fill, whether padding is billed, and whether the recipe does an extra reference or old-logprob pass ([Fireworks serverless docs](https://docs.fireworks.ai/fine-tuning/training-api/serverless)). Run a 2-3 step smoke test and read the meter before committing the budget.

Fireworks' Dedicated trainers cost **$8 per H100-hour**, which is not cheaper for a 30B model ([Fireworks pricing](https://fireworks.ai/pricing)). Together prices post-training only by GPU-hour, at $9/hr per B200 under a promotion ([Together](https://www.together.ai/pricing)). No Meta Llama model is in Fireworks' serverless training table ([Fireworks pricing](https://fireworks.ai/pricing)). So "Llama" means renting raw GPUs. The step counts for that route rest on our own FLOP estimate of 2-5 minutes per step for an 8B model on one H100. No published GRPO throughput figure was found.

**Recommendation: stay on Glimmer and Fireworks.** Spend the SFT budget, which is free, generously. Keep GRPO to one run of about 35 steps at about $60, with a $10 smoke test and $25-30 held back for evaluation sampling and a possible second pass. In-session sampling for evaluation is a separate paid line, and Glimmer's public serverless inference was deprecated on 2026-09-25 ([fw-ai/cookbook](https://github.com/fw-ai/cookbook)). The steelman for switching is real: 380 steps of Qwen3-8B on Tinker is a far stronger RL experiment. It answers a different question, though ("can RL teach a small model LEGO mechanisms"). SAMgo's demo needs Glimmer to do the editing, and six days does not leave room to rebuild the sampler, renderer and checkpoint pipeline for another model family.

## Prior work says SFT on ground truth first, then a gated, coupled reward

No paper has an LLM design LEGO Technic mechanisms that are checked for motion. A targeted search found none. SAMgo's "edit a build until a verifier says it works" sits in a gap. Four lines of work set the recipe.

**STABLE** is the closest generation recipe. It does full SFT of Qwen3.5-2B on 42,604 stable PointCloud2Brick structures, then TRL GRPO with **G=8, lr 5e-6 and temperature 0.7**. Its reward is additive: R_col + 5·IoU + 3·interlock + 2·connectivity, where the structural terms count only if the build is collision-free and in bounds. GRPO took the collision-free rate from **0.00 after SFT to 0.99**. RL from the base model scored **0.00**, because the base model rarely emitted parseable bricks. A shape-only reward was gamed with 1×1-brick spam, averaging 387 bricks ([STABLE](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06947)).

**PVPO** names the main failure for SAMgo: **PhysHack**. A physics-only reward reached validity 1.00 but voxel fidelity of only 0.29. A voxel-only reward dropped validity to 0.79 and fidelity to 0.08. The 50/50 coupled reward balanced them: validity 0.93, voxel 0.35, DINOv3 0.80 ([PVPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07602v2)). Its RL used LoRA rank 32 / alpha 64, lr 5e-6, G=8, KL 0.001 and 2k prompts over 6 epochs, about **96k rollouts**. That is roughly 20x what $100 buys on Glimmer.

**CAD-Coder** found that **8k high-quality SFT pairs beat 70k medium-quality pairs** (mean Chamfer distance 6.54 vs 9.89). It also found that a **geometry-only reward made the code invalid after about 200 GRPO steps**, which a format reward fixed ([CAD-Coder](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19713)).

**BesiegeField** is the only mechanism-building analogue. LLMs assemble wheels, hinges and springs in a physics sim. Two findings carry over. Parent-relative attachment beat absolute coordinates by a wide margin; Gemini 2.5 Pro scored 8.49 vs 4.96, and o3 and Claude Opus 4 produced 0/8 valid machines in coordinates. Simulator-state feedback beat scalar reward. Its GRPO used LoRA r64, KL 0.001 and 400 steps over about 48 hours. That run mostly raised best-of-N scores, not the mean, and it reduced diversity ([BesiegeField](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14980)).

**BrickNet** supplies the vocabulary. It types every connection as stud, hinge (with a flip), axle (with a slide), ball (with 2 extra rotational degrees of freedom) or fixed. It trained with SFT only and never tested whether anything moves ([BrickNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22984)).

Three implications for SAMgo follow. First, **the SFT data must be ground-truth fixes**, which is why corrupting verified builds is the right generator. All four successful pipelines used teacher or ground-truth data, and the project's self-sample SFT failed twice. Second, the reward needs a **hard gate on parse/apply, a "don't wreck the body" fidelity term, and per-check partial credit**. Otherwise the policy will either make tiny, trivially valid edits or spam parts. Third, edits should be expressed **relative to existing parts** ("axle through the holes of part #812", "hinge on the front edge of door group D1") rather than in raw coordinates where possible. MURPHY-style two-turn GRPO, in which failed outputs are re-prompted with executor feedback, raised Qwen3-1.7B HumanEval from 82.11 to 86.58 over plain GRPO, mostly when feedback was also used at inference ([MURPHY](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07833v2)). That matches the project's pilot finding that retries help. At this budget it belongs at inference, not in training.

Several points change the existing code. **The repo's rules sheet says the hole centre is "5.8 mm above its bottom". LDraw puts it at 14 LDU = 5.6 mm.** Pick one value and use it in both the verifier and the renderer, or the axles will look misplaced in the LDraw output. 5.8 mm is the figure often quoted for real bricks, and no primary source was found for it. The holes sit between the studs, so in the repo's stud-edge coordinates a hole lies on edge x+k. Stacking 1 brick + 2 plates (40 LDU) lines holes up vertically on a 2-stud pitch. A **stub axle** is then two round holes in parallel Technic bricks on one hole line, a 3-5L axle, and a wheel whose axle-shaped hole locks onto the axle, with a bush or stop-axle on the inside.

For **doors**, use 3821/3822 or the 2429/2430 swivel first. Their pivots are grid points on a vertical axis, so the "swings open without collision" check becomes a planar sweep of the door group's footprint about one corner. Brickset's rule that "the axis of the hinge has to be outside" the body is the same constraint the sweep enforces ([Brickset](https://brickset.com/article/100227/how-to-really-get-into-lego-buildings)). For **ball joints**, model ball centre = socket centre plus an orientation cone. No source gives the cone angle, so choose one (for example ±30-45°) and flag it. **Windows and colour regions** were not researched as parts. The cheapest correct verifier recolours body parts to a transparent colour from LDraw's `LDConfig.ldr` and checks that the window band is contiguous and lies between pillars. Clean colour is a region check: the number of connected colour components and the share of stray single-part islands. Neither needs new geometry.

## Base objects are easy; the scarce input is a verified completed design

For base meshes, **Objaverse 1.0 is the only zero-friction source**. It is ungated, installs with `pip install objaverse`, and is mostly CC-BY. Its LVIS annotations give **1,436 human-labelled road vehicles**, counted from the ungated `lvis-annotations.json.gz`: cars, police cruisers, race cars, pickups, buses, jeeps, trucks and more ([Objaverse docs](https://objaverse.allenai.org/docs/objaverse-1.0), [HF allenai/objaverse](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/objaverse)). Its GLBs are often not watertight, so voxelize with winding numbers or a flood-fill from outside, and filter scenes by part count and aspect ratio.

**ShapeNetCore v2** has about 3,514 cars and better meshes, but its HF repo needs **manual approval**: you give a name, a PI and an affiliation, and access is "considered for approval". Users report pending requests. It is also non-commercial only ([HF ShapeNetCore](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ShapeNet/ShapeNetCore), [SDF-SRN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.10505)). Request it today and do not plan around it. **DrivAerNet++** has 8,000 clean STL car bodies, but they are variants of one DrivAer sedan, CC BY-NC, and the full dataset is about 39 TB behind Globus ([DrivAerNet GitHub](https://github.com/Mohamedelrefaie/DrivAerNet)).

LEGO-native data adds little for the mechanism task. **StableText2Brick** (47,389 structures, MIT, ungated) is 1-plate-height bricks on a 20³ grid with no Technic parts ([HF StableText2Brick](https://huggingface.co/datasets/AvaLovelace/StableText2Brick)). The **LDraw OMR** has 1,470 official sets, but only one at a time, with no bulk zip ([LDraw OMR](https://library.ldraw.org/omr/sets)). Rebrickable forbids scraping MOC data ([Rebrickable terms](https://rebrickable.com/terms/)). BrickNet's 320K connector-typed samples are "available for research purposes" under unclear terms ([BrickNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22984)).

The real bottleneck is the **target side of each training pair**. Corruption only works if the uncorrupted build is a *completed, verified* mechanism design. For running gear, `car.py` already places gear algorithmically for cars up to a 16-stud through-axle. Extend it to stub axles and the wheel list, and it becomes the ground-truth generator for every size, including the 60-stud Camry. Doors need a programmatic door cutter: find the door panel between the pillars, split it into its own group, and add hinges on the front edge. It must pass the sweep verifier before its builds can serve as targets. About 300-500 base vehicles at mixed sizes, each corrupted 10-20 ways (remove the gear, swap round holes for axle holes, wrong wheel size, fuse a door into the body, opaque windows, speckled colour, remove supports), give the **5-10k ground-truth pairs** the literature's SFT sizes call for (CAD-Coder 8k, PVPO 11k).

## GRPO settings, reward and curriculum tuned for about 35 steps

The consensus small-run settings come from DAPO, Dr. GRPO, the Tinker docs, "LoRA Without Regret" and PVPO ([DAPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476v2), [Dr. GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20783), [Tinker RL](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tutorials/advanced/rl-hyperparams/), [LoRA Without Regret](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/)). For SAMgo they are:

- **Group size and batch:** G = 8 samples per prompt, 16 prompts per step.
- **Learning rate:** constant **1e-5** for the LoRA, with about 3 warmup steps. Fireworks' RFT default of 1e-4 is at the top of the stable range ([Fireworks RFT params](https://docs.fireworks.ai/fine-tuning/rft-parameters-reference)).
- **LoRA rank:** match the SFT adapter (8-32, all linear layers). Rank barely matters in RL, and MLP coverage does.
- **KL and clipping:** KL at 0 or 0.001, with clip-higher ε of 0.2 low / 0.28 high.
- **Loss and reward scaling:** token-level ("dapo") or "dr_grpo" loss, with no per-group standard-deviation scaling. Batch-level or no scaling keeps tiny similarity differences from becoming large advantages.
- **Sampling:** temperature 1.0 and entropy bonus 0, adding 0.001 only if entropy collapses. Use μ = 1, meaning one update per batch.
- **Output length:** a maximum of about 1.2 × the longest legitimate SFT edit list, with truncated outputs masked rather than scored as zero.

If budget forces it, G=4 roughly halves the train meter, giving about 125 steps per $100 at 1k-token prompts, at the cost of noisier advantages (our arithmetic). Prompts with constant reward across the group give no gradient. Filter them, as DAPO's dynamic sampling does; that was DAPO's largest single gain, +8 AIME points.

The **reward** follows PVPO and CAD-Coder, with STABLE's gating:

- **Gate:** if the JSON fails to parse or an edit cannot be applied, R = −0.5.
- **Body-integrity gate:** if the edited body's shape match drops more than a set amount below the input (for example from 99.5% to under 98%), or more parts are removed than the task allows, R = 0. This is the PhysHack and part-spam guard.
- **Otherwise:** R = 0.5 × (fraction of hard checks passed) + 0.5 × match-to-completed-design. The match term is piecewise-linear with a floor, in the style of CAD-Coder. The checks are per mechanism: round holes, two aligned holes per stub axle, wheel size in range, wheels clear the body while turning, wheels are the lowest contact, door group attached only through hinges, a shared pivot, a collision-free sweep to about 70°, stability with the door open and closed, and the window and colour region checks.
- **Bonus:** +0.2 only when every hard check passes and the match clears a threshold.

MO-GRPO shows that the advantage drifts toward whichever term has the higher variance, so log each component and normalize it if one dominates ([MO-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22047v1)). Log the parse rate, each check's pass rate, the match, the edit count, entropy and the share of zero-variance groups every step. Stop if the parse rate falls or the edit count shrinks while reward rises.

The **curriculum** is a reverse curriculum by corruption size, in the style of R3 ([R3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05808)). Start with one or two removed or broken parts and grow as each bucket's pass rate passes about 70%. Before RL, sample each candidate prompt 8 times with the SFT model. Keep prompts whose full-pass rate is between about 10% and 90%; this window is common practice, not a tuned number. 1-shot RLVR shows that a small set of high-variance prompts can carry RL, and that unsolvable prompts teach nothing ([1-shot RLVR](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20571)). Order the mechanisms by verifier readiness and demo value: running gear first (the verifier exists), then doors, then windows and colour, then stability. Ball joints come last and get SFT only, since the Camry demo does not need them.

**Flagged uncertainties:**

- **Fireworks billing:** padding, the shared prompt across the 8 samples, and possible extra logprob passes could raise the per-step cost by 15-50%.

## Conclusion

The $100 cap changes what the RL stage is for. On Glimmer it cannot be the main training signal: about 35 steps against PVPO's 96k rollouts. What it can do is test whether the coupled, gated reward moves a model that free SFT has already made competent. The real decision is therefore not Glimmer versus Llama but **where the effort goes**: into the corruption generator and the verifiers, which make free SFT and inference-time retries strong, rather than into RL infrastructure. That allocation also gives SAMgo something the field lacks. No prior LEGO work, BrickNet included, checks that a hinge swings or a wheel spins. A verifier suite that does is reusable as a benchmark whether or not the 35 RL steps move the needle.

Two cheap moves keep the plan resilient. First, shrinking prompts to local crops is the single biggest cost lever, and it probably also helps accuracy, since BesiegeField's results favour local, parent-relative edits over global coordinates. Second, the Camry's stability failure must be fixed before any model work. It is the one blocker no amount of training can route around.
